Frozen food began with a fish on the ice.
Labrador, 1912.
Clarence Birdseye, a fur trader, watched Inuit fishermen leave their catch on the ice.
It froze at once.
Cooked later, it tasted fresh.
Frozen food in shops tasted like mush.
It froze slowly, in large ice crystals.
Birdseye copied the cold.
He built a freezer that pressed food between chilled belts.
His first company ran out of money.
He kept going.
1930, Springfield, Massachusetts, his frozen food reached the shops.
Home refrigerators rose from under 10 percent to over 50.
