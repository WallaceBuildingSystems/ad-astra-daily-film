# Clarence Birdseye, quick freezing (research, 18 October 2026, James's session)

Selection test: 1 One person owns the idea (Birdseye). 2 Zero to one: no one had sold fast-frozen packaged food. 3 It changed how we live: food kept fresh out of season, home freezers. 4 Lasting: frozen food is a permanent part of daily life. 5 Not a family firm story.

## Facts used, with sources
- Labrador, 1912 to about 1917 (Wikipedia gives 1912 to 1915, intermittent; History.com gives 1912 to 1917). The film says only "1912". He worked as a fur trader and fox breeder. Source: History.com https://history.com/news/history-of-frozen-food-birdseye
- Observation: Inuit fishermen ice fished and let the catch freeze immediately in the cold air; when cooked later it tasted fresh. Source: History.com. The temperature differs by source (minus 30, minus 40), so the film gives none.
- Problem: commercially frozen food then froze too slowly, forming large ice crystals that damaged texture and flavour. Source: History.com.
- Method: freezing between two chilled belts under pressure, in waxed cardboard packs. Sources: Supermarket News Hall of Fame 2012; Wikipedia (pressure plates, patent 1,773,079, 12 August 1930).
- First company failed: a frozen-fish company founded in 1922 or 1923 ran out of money (History.com; Wikipedia gives bankruptcy in 1924). The film says only that his first company ran out of money. General Seafoods followed in Gloucester, Massachusetts.
- 1930: the first retail frozen products were sold in Springfield, Massachusetts (National Inventors Hall of Fame https://invent.org/inductees/clarence-birdseye; Mass Moments 6 March 1930 https://massmoments.org/moment-details/clarence-birdseye-tests-frozen-food.html). Store and product counts differ by source, so the film gives none.
- Ordinary-life number: U.S. refrigerator ownership rose from less than 10 percent to well over 50 percent, with his freezing innovations credited with helping spur demand. Source: History.com (no date given in the article).
- Not used: the 1929 sale for 22 million dollars, the 200 patents, the frozen food market size (weak source).

## Failed attempts list
First company ran out of money; earlier commercially frozen food was mushy (slow freezing).
