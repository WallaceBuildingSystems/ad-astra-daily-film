The seat belt that saves lives began with an ejection seat engineer.
Sweden, 1958.
Nils Bohlin built ejection seats for pilots at Saab.
Volvo hired him as a safety engineer.
Waist-only belts left heads hitting the dash and the wheel.
Many people still did not wear them.
He spent almost a year on a belt that held the chest and the hips.
It was easy to fasten and easy to release.
1959, the first Volvos were sold with it.
Volvo let every other car maker use the design for free.
Germany's patent office named it 1 of its 8 most important patents in 100 years.
In the United States it saves an estimated 15,000 lives a year.
2004, Germany required it on every seat.
