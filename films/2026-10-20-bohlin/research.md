# Nils Bohlin, the three-point seat belt (research, 20 October 2026, James's session)

Selection test: 1 One person owns the idea (Bohlin). 2 Zero to one: no belt held chest and hips with 1 strap. 3 Changed how we live: every car. 4 Lasting: still the standard design.

## Facts used, with sources
- Bohlin (1920 to 2002) designed ejection seats at Saab; joined Volvo as a safety engineer in 1958. Sources: Automotive Hall of Fame https://www.automotivehalloffame.org/?p=483 ; German Patent and Trade Mark Office https://dpma.de/english/our_office/publications/milestones/inventionsthatmadehistory/safetybelt/index.html ; Wikipedia.
- Waist-only belts were linked to facial injuries from hitting the dash or wheel, whiplash and internal injuries; many people did not wear belts. Source: The Collector https://www.thecollector.com/inventor-nils-bohlin-change-auto-industry/ (secondary).
- Almost a year to finish the design. Sources: The Collector; Wikipedia (about a year).
- Easy to fasten and release, holds upper and lower body. Source: German Patent and Trade Mark Office.
- 1959, first Volvos sold with it (PV544 per The Collector; Wikipedia says introduced 1959). The film says only "the first Volvos".
- Volvo made the design free for other makers. Sources: The Collector; Wikipedia (free for public use, year given as 1968 there, so the film gives no year).
- 1985 International Patent Conference, Munich: the West German patent office named it 1 of its 8 most important patents in its first 100 years. Source: Automotive Hall of Fame.
- Estimated 15,000 lives a year in the United States, National Highway Traffic Safety Administration figure as cited by Wikipedia (not opened directly; flagged).
- 1 July 2004: three-point belts compulsory on all seats in Germany. Source: German Patent and Trade Mark Office.
- Not used: patent filing dates (Swedish 29 August 1958 and United States 1959 sources differ), the 1 million lives claim (unsourced), Volvo Amazon as first car.
