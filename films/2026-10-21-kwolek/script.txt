A cloudy liquid that looked like a failure became a fibre stronger than steel.
Delaware, 1965.
Stephanie Kwolek had been a DuPont chemist since 1946.
Her new solution came out thin and cloudy.
Everyone assumed it had failed.
The technician refused to spin it, fearing it would clog the machine.
She insisted.
The fibre came out about 5 times stronger than steel by weight.
Kevlar reached the market in 1971.
Today it goes into about 200 products, from tyres to boat hulls.
By 2006, police body armour had saved at least 3,000 officers.
Kwolek retired in 1986 and died in 2014, aged 90.
The failure was the discovery.
