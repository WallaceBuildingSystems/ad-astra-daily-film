# Harold Ridley, the intraocular lens (research, 17 October 2026, opened in James's session)

Selection test: 1 One person owns the idea (Ridley, made with the optician John Pike of Rayner and the chemist John Holt of Imperial Chemical Industries). 2 Zero to one: no lens had ever been left in a human eye. 3 Lasting: a lens implant is the standard treatment for cataract today. 4 Results: 6 to 10 million cataract operations a year worldwide.

## Facts used, with sources
- Before: cataract surgery removed the clouded lens completely (from the 1740s); without a lens vision was blurred and patients wore thick spectacles. Source: Science Museum Group blog, https://blog.sciencemuseum.org.uk/a-visionary-innovation-harold-ridley-and-the-first-intra-ocular-surgery/
- The idea: in the Second World War, slivers of Perspex from aircraft cockpit canopies lodged in pilots' eyes and were not rejected (Ridley treated Squadron Leader Gordon "Mouse" Cleaver, 601 Squadron, in 1940). Sources: Ridley Eye Foundation, https://ridleyeyefoundation.org/our-history/ ; St John's Wood Memories page, https://www.stjohnswoodmemories.org.uk/content/new-contributions/sir-harold-ridley-1906-2001-pioneer-of-cataracts ; Royal College of Surgeons Lives, https://livesonline.rcseng.ac.uk/biogs/E008877b.htm (born 10 July 1906, aged 43 at the first implant; same Perspex as the Hawker Hurricane fighters).
- 1947: a medical student, Steve Parry, asked during a cataract operation why the lens could not be replaced. Source: Science Museum Group blog.
- Maker: John Pike of Rayner made the lens, John Holt of Imperial Chemical Industries made clinical-grade Perspex (Perspex C.Q.). Source: Science Museum Group blog; Rayner, "Invention of the IOL", https://rayner.com/global/wp-content/uploads/sites/21/2021/12/Invention_of_the_IOL.pdf (David J. Apple's account).
- First operation 29 November 1949 at St Thomas' Hospital, London, a 45-year-old woman, done in two stages (Rayner/Apple). The St John's Wood page says a lens was first left permanently in an eye on 8 February 1950; the Science Museum says it is unclear whether the lens went in at the first operation. The film says "29 November 1949" for the first operation and does not claim the lens went in that day.
- What failed first: the first lens was too thick (more than 14 diopters of short-sightedness); after the second implant at Moorfields in 1950 the power was recalculated to about 1 diopter. Source: Rayner/Apple.
- Reaction: peers condemned it as reckless; Oxford Ophthalmological Congress 9 October 1951 reception was mixed and his film was never shown; no patent. Sources: Royal College of Surgeons Lives; Science Museum Group blog.
- Ridley implanted about 1,000 lenses; he was knighted in 2000. Sources: Science Museum Group blog; Ridley Eye Foundation.
- Today: cataract surgery with a lens implant is the most frequently performed operation worldwide, 6 to 10 million a year (no year given). Source: PubMed Central review, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2780012
- Still used: lens implantation is the standard treatment; the original acrylic (polymethylmethacrylate) remains in use.

## Failed attempts list
First lens too thick (above), a share of early lenses dislocated or were removed (up to 20 per cent, Science Museum), sterilising agent absorbed by the plastic (Rayner/Apple).
