A kidney machine built from sausage skins.
1943, German-occupied Netherlands.
Kidney failure meant death within days.
Willem Kolff, doctor, aged 32.
He wound cellophane sausage casing around a rotating drum.
Blood ran through the casing. The drum turned in a tank of fluid.
Waste passed out. Clean blood returned.
14 of his first 15 patients died.
September 1945, patient 17.
A 67-year-old woman, in a coma.
After hours on the machine, she woke.
She lived 7 more years.
Today, 1.5 million people are on dialysis.
Each treatment is still his idea.
