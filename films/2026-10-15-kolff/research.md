# Willem Kolff, artificial kidney, research (15 October 2026)

Selection test: 1 owner of the core idea (Kolff), zero to one (no machine could clean blood before 1943), lasting (dialysis is still how kidney failure is treated), ordinary-life number (about 1.5 million people on chronic dialysis).

Facts used, with sources opened this run:
- Kampen, German-occupied Netherlands, 1943 to 1945; cellophane tubing from sausage casing wound on a horizontal drum turning in an enamel tank of rinsing fluid; aluminium strips replaced by wooden slats when metal ran out; heparin as anticoagulant. Source: Lasker Foundation, https://laskerfoundation.org/winners/hemodialysis-for-treating-end-stage-kidney-disease/ and Kolff's own account, https://laskerfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2002_kolff.pdf
- "Of the first 15 patients treated in the next 2 years, only 1 survived" (Kolff, Lasker Foundation 2002), so 14 of 15 died. The Lasker Foundation winner page says 16 patients 1943 to 1944 with limited success.
- Seventeenth patient, September 1945: a 67-year-old woman in uremic coma from acute kidney failure; after about 11 hours of dialysis she regained consciousness and her kidneys made urine; she lived 7 more years (Lasker Foundation winner page). Kolff's own paper says 8 hours; the script says "hours" only.
- Today: about 1.5 million people on chronic dialysis worldwide (Lasker Foundation winner page); Kolff's 2002 paper says more than 1 million. Script uses 1.5 million.
- Age 32 in 1943: born 14 February 1911, Leiden (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Johan_Kolff). Birth date not on the institutional pages, so the film says "aged 32" only.
- National WWII Museum, https://www.nationalww2museum.org/node/53520: Kampen hospital, kidney kept hidden from German authorities, gravity moved the blood, juice cans and washing machine parts.
- Kidney Care UK, https://kidneycareuk.org/kidney-disease-information/treatments/dialysis-80-years-of-the-artificial-kidney/: sausage skins, orange juice cans, washing machine parts.
