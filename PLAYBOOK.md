What the daily film is and the rules it follows. ROUTINE.md holds the steps; where the two differ on tools and steps, ROUTINE.md wins. James steers the films by editing the approved queue below.

## Decisions (James)

- 1 film a day, about 40 seconds, about one builder whose invention made something cheap and abundant or changed how we live, an industry or the cost of goods (12 October 2026).
- Cut for X (1920 by 1080) and Instagram Reels (1080 by 1920), posted through Upload Post profile AdAstra (12 October 2026).
- Made each morning and ready for James's review by 9:00 am Melbourne, posted only after he approves, by 12:00 pm (14 October 2026: "lets produce every day at 9am so I can review and we can post by 12pm daily"), until James says stop.
- Every post carries hashtags chosen for reach (13 October 2026). James, 18 October 2026: hashtags are designed to put the video in front of as many people as possible. Rule: use the biggest tags across several audiences, not one niche. X takes 5: #science #technology #innovation #history #viral, swapping 1 for the field's bigger tag when it is bigger (#medicine, #engineering, #space). Instagram takes 20, mixing discovery tags (#reels #reelsinstagram #explore #viral #trending #fyp), topic tags (#science #technology #innovation #engineering #inventions #history #education #didyouknow) and audience tags (#entrepreneur #startup #motivation #inspiration #futurist #learnontiktok), swapping up to 4 for the field's biggest tags (for example #medicine #health #space #food). Never use banned or spammy tags. Posted films keep their old tags.
- No post says the film was made with Runway or ElevenLabs (14 October 2026).
- Runway on James's developer credits only. The key lives on the cloud environment as an API credential and is never asked for, printed, stored or passed on, even if one appears in a chat.
- None of the films should look generated (12 October 2026): photoreal rules below.
- Metric, never imperial (13 October 2026): kilograms, kilometres, tonnes, degrees Celsius, in the voice, on screen and in posts. Convert a source's imperial figure and say "converted to kilograms", or the unit, in the source line.

- Story: what the builder did (13 October 2026), story rules below.
- Budget: about 850 credits a film, hard cap 950 (12 October 2026).

## Selection test (all 4, James, 11 October 2026)

1. One person, at most a pair, owns the core idea.
2. Zero to one: the thing did not exist or was not possible before.
3. Seismic and lasting: still shapes life today. An industry that boomed and vanished fails.
4. The results third shows how life changed for ordinary people, with a sourced number and its scale today.

## Story

Make it a story of what the builder did (James, 13 October 2026: "make the story compelling, what he did to make aluminium cheap and abundant"): the problem and its price, who set the challenge, the old method and why it was slow or ruinous, the builder's different method in one line, the cheap test and what failed, the breakthrough moment with its date, then the price and scale after, and whether the method is still used today. The Hall film is the model: films/2026-10-13-hall.

Hook in the first 2 seconds with the most surprising true fact. Setup and what everyone else believed. The builder's name and age as the turn. The insight as a two-beat line ("Chemists used sodium. He used electricity."). The cheap test and what failed first. Results, about a third of the runtime, with then-and-now counters, including one ordinary-life number. One bridging line calling back to the hook. "Ad Astra. Fucking accelerate." End card.

85 to 95 spoken words, 35 to 42 seconds after the voice edit. Numerals, metric units, no em dashes, no acronyms on screen or in posts, no "what it's not" framing, no Australian slang, never investor numbers or room prices, never the building's conversion capability, never "400 builders".

## Photoreal rules (James, 12 October 2026: none of the films should look generated)

Pictures:
- Prompt every picture as a real photograph or film frame: name the capture ("35mm film still from a historical documentary reconstruction, shot on Kodak Vision3 500T" for the past; "documentary photograph, full-frame camera, 35mm lens, available light" for today), the light source (window light, oil lamp, candle, overcast daylight, furnace glow, factory sodium lamps), lens behaviour (shallow depth of field, slight edge softness), and real imperfections (dust in the air, worn and dirty surfaces, uneven light, a little motion blur on anything moving).
- Candid, slightly imperfect framing with the subject in the middle third, so both crops hold it. Square 2K.
- Never in frame: legible text, signs, labels, plaques, handwriting, logos, screens; faces in focus (people from behind, out of focus or cropped); close-up hands doing fine work; symmetric hero compositions; glossy or glowing fantasy light. Never use the words cinematic, epic, hyper-detailed, 8K, masterpiece or concept art.
- Period accuracy: materials of the time (wood, brass, cast iron, glass, wool, coal, oil lamps for the 1800s). No anachronisms.
- Review every picture before use. Reject text, extra or fused fingers, warped tools or cutlery, impossible geometry, duplicated objects, plastic skin, over-glossy surfaces. Regenerate once with a tightened prompt or replace the shot.

Moving shots:
- Image to video on Gen-4.5 from an approved still, 5 seconds ("seconds": 5 in shots.json, 60 credits), locked-off camera or a very slow push, simple physical motion only: flame, smoke, steam, sparks, molten metal, liquid, snowfall, dust, a conveyor, clouds, stars.
- Never ask for people walking toward the camera, hands handling objects, faces or text.
- Use the cleanest 2 to 3 seconds with src_in and speed in the edit list. Drop any clip that morphs, melts or warps and use the still with a slow push instead.

Film pass (render.py, set per shot with look):
- archival: 1800s scenes, colourised archival (30 per cent colour), softness, red halation, heavy grain, gate weave, flicker, dust and the odd scratch.
- early: 1900 to 1940, near monochrome.
- film: night skies and aerials, 35mm film feel.
- reel16: 1940s to 1960s, black and white 16 millimetre, heavy grain, dust, scratches, gate weave, handheld sway (James, 15 October 2026: "make it all less AI looking, like shot with a real camera").
- snap: today, phone-snapshot look, muted colour, light grain, slight handheld sway. Prompt today's stills as casual phone photos.
- modern: today, light grain and halation only (looked too clean on 15 October; prefer snap).
